# Leave and Absence Policy

This policy sets out the leave entitlements and absence procedures for all employees. Entitlements are minimums; individual employment contracts may specify greater entitlements, in which case the contract prevails. All leave must be requested and tracked through the HR system (HRConnect).

## Annual Leave

Full-time employees are entitled to 25 days of paid annual leave per calendar year, plus Swiss public holidays (or the equivalent in other countries for employees based outside Switzerland). Part-time employees receive a pro-rata entitlement based on their contracted hours.

The leave year runs from 1 January to 31 December. Employees hired part-way through the year receive a pro-rata entitlement for the remainder of the year.

### Requesting Annual Leave

Leave requests must be submitted in HRConnect at least five business days in advance for absences of up to three days, and at least three weeks in advance for absences of four days or more. Managers must respond to requests within three business days. The company reserves the right to decline leave requests based on business need, but must give a reason and help the employee find an alternative window.

### Carryover

Up to five days of unused annual leave may be carried into the following year and must be used by 31 March. Leave not taken and not carried over is forfeited and not compensated in cash, except upon termination of employment.

### Leave During Notice Period

Annual leave may be taken during a notice period only with manager approval. If an employee has excess untaken leave upon departure that cannot be scheduled, it will be paid out at the employee's daily rate.

## Sick Leave

Employees who are unable to work due to illness or injury receive paid sick leave as follows:

- Up to 5 consecutive days: no medical certificate required, but the absence must be logged in HRConnect by the end of the first day.
- 6 or more consecutive days: a medical certificate (doctor's note) must be submitted to HR by day six. The company may also require a fitness-for-work certificate before return after a long absence.
- Accumulated sick leave exceeding 10 days in a rolling 12-month period: HR will initiate a supportive discussion to understand whether the employee needs additional support or occupational health referral.

Paid sick leave is provided at full salary for up to 730 days (two years) in accordance with Swiss law. Employees who exceed this threshold may be eligible for long-term disability benefits under the company's insurance policy — HR will advise on this individually.

### Sick Leave and Annual Leave

Annual leave cannot be converted to sick leave retrospectively. However, if an employee falls ill during a period of already-approved annual leave and obtains a medical certificate covering those days, the affected days will be restored as annual leave and rescheduled.

## Parental Leave

### Maternity Leave

Employees who have given birth are entitled to 16 weeks of paid maternity leave at full salary, starting from the date of birth. At least eight weeks must be taken immediately after birth; the remaining eight weeks may be taken in a block or, with HR agreement, split into shorter periods within 12 months of the birth.

### Paternity / Partner Leave

The non-birthing parent or a partner (including same-sex partners) is entitled to 10 working days of paid leave to be taken within six months of the birth or adoption.

### Adoption Leave

An employee who adopts a child under the age of 10 is entitled to 16 weeks of paid adoption leave at full salary, mirroring the maternity leave provisions. Both parents may not take adoption leave simultaneously unless agreed in advance with their managers.

### Parental Leave (extended, unpaid)

Beyond the paid provisions above, employees may request up to 26 additional weeks of unpaid parental leave within the first 18 months of a child's birth or adoption. Requests must be made in writing at least eight weeks before the intended start date. The position is held open; seniority and benefits continue to accrue during unpaid leave, but salary is not paid.

## Compassionate Leave

Employees are entitled to paid compassionate leave in the following circumstances:

- Death of a spouse, civil partner, or child: 5 days
- Death of a parent, sibling, or parent-in-law: 3 days
- Death of a grandparent or close friend at the manager's discretion: up to 1 day
- Serious illness of an immediate family member requiring emergency care: up to 2 days

Additional unpaid compassionate leave may be granted at the manager's discretion in exceptional circumstances. Employees should not feel pressured to return to work before they are ready; HR and EAP (Employee Assistance Programme) support is available.

## Public Duties and Jury Service

Employees called for jury service or to perform other civic duties (such as serving as an elected local councillor) are entitled to take the necessary time off. The company pays full salary for the first 10 days and the difference between the civic allowance and normal salary for up to a further 20 days. Longer periods are treated as unpaid leave or may be covered by annual leave.

Jury summons or other notices must be shown to HR as soon as received, and the company will apply for deferral only if the employee's absence would cause genuine operational hardship.

## Study and Exam Leave

Employees undertaking an approved professional qualification or study programme relevant to their role may take up to 10 days of paid study leave per year for study, preparation, and sitting of examinations. Approval must be obtained from the manager and HR before the study programme begins, not retrospectively.

## Unpaid Leave

Employees may apply for up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave in any 24-month period for personal reasons such as extended travel, voluntary work, or family care beyond the provisions above. Requests must be made at least four weeks in advance. Approval is at the manager's discretion and is not guaranteed. Pension contributions and benefits accrual pause during unpaid leave.

## Return to Work

After any absence of five or more consecutive days, the manager will conduct a brief return-to-work conversation. This is supportive, not disciplinary, and ensures the employee is genuinely fit to return and that any adjustments needed are identified early. Notes of the conversation are kept in HRConnect and are confidential.

## Absence Without Notice

Failure to contact the company on the first day of an unplanned absence, or failure to maintain communication during an extended absence, may be treated as unauthorised absence and result in loss of pay for the affected days and, if persistent, disciplinary action.

Questions about any aspect of leave entitlements should be directed to hr@company.com.
