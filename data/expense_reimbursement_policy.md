# Expense Reimbursement Policy

This policy governs how employees submit, approve, and receive reimbursement for business-related expenses incurred on behalf of the company. It applies to all full-time employees, part-time staff, and contractors unless their contract specifies otherwise.

## General Principles

The company reimburses reasonable and necessary expenses that are directly related to conducting company business. "Reasonable" means the amount an ordinary prudent person would spend in similar circumstances. Employees are expected to seek cost-effective options and avoid unnecessary spending. Personal expenses, even if incurred during a business trip, are not reimbursable.

All expenses must be submitted with original receipts. Credit card statements alone are not acceptable as proof of expenditure. Receipts must clearly show the vendor, date, itemised amounts, and — for meals — the attendees and business purpose.

## Travel Expenses

### Flights and Rail

Economy class is required for all flights under six hours. Business class may be approved in advance by the department head for flights exceeding six hours, or where a medical condition documented by a physician makes economy class medically unsuitable. Employees should book at least 14 days in advance to obtain lower fares. Booking less than 7 days in advance without prior written approval from a manager may result in partial reimbursement.

Train travel should be booked in standard class. First class is permitted when standard class is sold out or when the journey exceeds four hours and a seat reservation in standard class is unavailable.

### Accommodation

Hotel accommodation is reimbursed up to the following nightly limits, inclusive of taxes and fees:

- Switzerland: CHF 250 per night
- Western Europe (excluding Switzerland): EUR 200 per night
- North America: USD 250 per night
- Asia-Pacific: USD 200 per night
- All other regions: USD 150 per night

Employees who choose accommodation above these limits pay the difference themselves. Airbnb and similar platforms are permitted provided the stay is documented with a booking confirmation and receipt.

### Ground Transportation

Taxi and ride-share (Uber, Lyft) expenses are reimbursable when public transport is impractical — for example, late at night, when carrying heavy equipment, or when the journey would exceed 60 minutes by public transport. Employees should choose the economy tier of ride-share services; premium tiers require justification.

Rental cars are permitted when a car is genuinely necessary for the work. Employees should choose compact or economy class vehicles. Fuel, tolls, and parking directly related to business travel are reimbursable. Fines and traffic violations are not.

Personal car use is reimbursed at CHF 0.70 per kilometre (Switzerland) or the applicable statutory rate in other countries. The starting point is the employee's normal workplace, not their home.

### Meals While Travelling

Daily meal limits while on overnight business travel:

- Breakfast: CHF 20 / EUR 18 / USD 20
- Lunch: CHF 30 / EUR 28 / USD 30
- Dinner: CHF 60 / EUR 55 / USD 60

These limits are per person and include non-alcoholic beverages. Alcohol is not reimbursable unless it is part of a client entertainment meal approved under the Entertainment section below.

## Client Entertainment

Meals and events with clients, prospects, or key partners may be reimbursed when the primary purpose is advancing a business relationship. The following conditions apply:

1. The expense must be approved by a manager before the event if it is expected to exceed CHF 300 total.
2. The receipt must be accompanied by a brief written explanation of the business purpose and a list of attendees with their company names.
3. The per-person limit for client meals is CHF 150. Expenditure above this requires VP-level approval.
4. Alcohol at client dinners is reimbursable within the overall per-person limit.

## Home Office Equipment

Employees approved for permanent remote work may claim reimbursement for home office setup costs up to CHF 1,500 as a one-time allowance, covering items such as a desk, ergonomic chair, monitor stand, or keyboard. This allowance resets every four years. Items purchased remain the employee's property and do not need to be returned when employment ends.

An annual ergonomic consumables allowance of CHF 200 covers items such as a mouse, mousepad, desk lamp, or headset. Internet costs are not reimbursable.

## Submission Deadlines

Expense reports must be submitted within 30 calendar days of the expense being incurred. Expenses submitted after 30 days require manager approval and a written explanation. Expenses submitted more than 90 days after the date of expenditure will not be reimbursed under any circumstances, as they cannot be recorded in the correct accounting period.

## Approval Workflow

Expenses up to CHF 500 per report are approved by the employee's direct manager. Expenses between CHF 500 and CHF 2,000 require the manager's approval plus finance team review. Expenses above CHF 2,000 require additional approval from the department VP. The finance team processes approved reports within 10 business days and pays reimbursements via the employee's registered bank account.

## Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following are explicitly not reimbursable regardless of circumstance:

- Personal grooming, clothing, or luggage (unless luggage is lost or damaged by a carrier during a business trip)
- Gym, spa, or leisure activities
- In-flight WiFi for personal use
- Minibar charges
- Entertainment for personal guests who are not business contacts
- Speeding fines, parking tickets, or other violations
- Gifts to colleagues (see the Gifts Policy for client gifts)
- Political donations

## Policy Violations

Submitting a fraudulent expense claim — including falsified receipts, inflated amounts, or claims for expenses not incurred — is grounds for disciplinary action up to and including termination, and may result in criminal referral. The company conducts periodic audits of expense reports. Employees found to have submitted improper claims must repay the amount in full.
