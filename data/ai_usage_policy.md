# Artificial Intelligence Usage Policy

This policy governs how employees may use AI tools — including large language models (LLMs), generative AI assistants, AI-powered coding assistants, image generators, and similar technologies — in their work. It applies to all employees, contractors, and interns. As AI tooling evolves rapidly, this policy is reviewed every six months and employees are notified of updates.

## Purpose

AI tools can meaningfully accelerate certain work: drafting, summarisation, code generation, translation, brainstorming, and data analysis. The company encourages their responsible use where they genuinely help. At the same time, AI systems introduce risks — data leakage, inaccuracy, copyright infringement, and over-reliance — that employees must understand and manage.

## Approved Tools

The IT and Security teams maintain a list of approved AI tools on the intranet (AI Tools Register). Tools on this list have been reviewed for data privacy compliance, security, and acceptable terms of service.

Employees must not use unapproved AI tools for any work that involves company data, customer data, or confidential information. Using a personal AI account (e.g., a free-tier consumer subscription) for company work is not permitted, because data submitted to consumer-tier services may be used to train the provider's models. The company provides enterprise-tier access to approved tools, which contractually excludes training use.

To propose a new tool for approval, submit a request to it-security@company.com. The review process takes approximately two weeks.

## What You May Not Input into AI Systems

Regardless of whether the tool is approved, you must never submit the following to any external AI service:

- **Personal data of employees, customers, or any individuals** — names paired with contact details, salary data, health information, financial account details, or any information that would constitute Restricted data under the Information Security Policy.
- **Unpublished financial results** — revenue figures, forecasts, or any other non-public financial information.
- **Customer contract terms, pricing, or SLAs** — these are Confidential and may give competitors insight if exposed.
- **Source code of proprietary products** — unless IT Security has specifically approved that the coding assistant operates in an isolated mode that does not retain or transmit code externally.
- **Trade secrets or patentable inventions** — submitting these to an external LLM may compromise patent applications or constitute disclosure.
- **Legal advice received from counsel** — attorney-client privilege may be waived by disclosure to third-party AI services.

When in doubt, anonymise or aggregate data before submitting, or ask your manager or Legal.

## Accuracy and Verification

AI-generated content must be reviewed and verified before being used in any external communication, document submitted to a regulator, customer-facing material, or internal decision. AI systems hallucinate — they generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect output with no indication that they are wrong. Employees are accountable for the accuracy of everything they send or sign off, regardless of whether AI assisted in producing it.

Specific guidance by use case:

- **Legal or compliance documents**: AI may assist with drafting, but Legal must review and approve all final versions. Never submit AI-generated legal text without review.
- **Code**: AI-generated code must be reviewed for security vulnerabilities, correctness, and licensing issues before merging. Security should be given particular attention — AI models are known to suggest insecure patterns.
- **Financial figures**: Never include AI-generated numbers in financial reports without verification against source data.
- **Citations and references**: AI tools frequently fabricate citations — academic papers, case law, and URLs that do not exist. Verify every reference independently before using it.

## Intellectual Property and Copyright

The copyright status of AI-generated output is unsettled across jurisdictions. As a precaution:

- Do not represent AI-generated content as entirely original creative work in contexts where originality is legally significant (e.g., copyright registrations, academic submissions).
- Be aware that AI training data includes third-party copyrighted material, and AI outputs may occasionally reproduce substantial portions of that material. If you suspect a generated piece of text or image is reproduced from a copyrighted source, do not use it without seeking Legal guidance.
- Code generated by AI assistants may be derived from open-source code with licence conditions (e.g., GPL, AGPL) that impose requirements on use and distribution. The engineering team has guidance on licence compliance for AI-generated code.

## Transparency

Employees must be transparent about the use of AI in their work when:

- Asked directly by a manager, client, or colleague whether AI was used.
- Submitting academic work under any arrangement that prohibits AI use.
- Publishing content where the audience has a reasonable expectation of human authorship (e.g., a personal thought-leadership article published under your name).

Employees are not required to proactively disclose AI use in routine internal work (drafting, summarising, coding) unless their team or department has established a specific disclosure norm.

## Prohibited Uses

The following uses of AI tools are prohibited:

- Generating content intended to deceive, manipulate, or defraud anyone.
- Creating deepfakes or synthetic media of real individuals without their consent.
- Attempting to circumvent safety filters or content moderation on AI systems (jailbreaking).
- Using AI to conduct or facilitate surveillance, profiling, or discrimination based on protected characteristics.
- Automating communications to clients or external parties without disclosure that the communication is AI-generated, where this would be materially deceptive.

## Agentic and Automated AI Systems

Some AI use cases involve AI systems that act autonomously — browsing the web, executing code, sending emails, or modifying files — rather than simply generating text for human review. These "agentic" uses require additional approval from the AI Governance Committee before deployment, because the potential for irreversible actions is much higher.

Any agentic system deployed with company data or on the company's behalf must have a human review step for any irreversible action (sending external communications, executing financial transactions, modifying production data) and must log all actions for audit purposes.

## Questions and Reporting

Questions about whether a specific use case is permitted should be directed to ai-policy@company.com. Concerns about potential misuse of AI tools by colleagues should be raised through the standard whistleblowing channel. The company will not penalise good-faith queries about what is permitted.
