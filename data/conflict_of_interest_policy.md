# Conflict of Interest Policy

A conflict of interest arises when an employee's personal interests — financial, relational, or otherwise — could impair, or appear to impair, their ability to act in the company's best interest. This policy supplements the obligations set out in the Code of Conduct and provides detailed guidance on identifying conflicts, the disclosure procedure, cooling-off obligations, and the role of the Ethics Committee.

## What Constitutes a Conflict of Interest

Conflicts of interest may be actual, potential, or perceived. Common situations requiring disclosure include holding a financial interest in a supplier, customer, or competitor; directing company business to a vendor in which a family member holds a stake; participating in a hiring or performance decision involving a close family member or romantic partner; pursuing outside employment that competes with or materially diverts time from your primary role; and serving on the board of a for-profit organisation without prior written approval. The appearance of a conflict can be as damaging as an actual one. Employees should disclose any situation that a reasonable colleague might perceive as compromising their objectivity, even where the employee believes their own judgment to be unaffected.

## Gifts, Hospitality, and Vendor Relationships

Modest gifts and hospitality are a normal part of business relationships but require careful management when they involve parties with whom the company does business. Employees may receive gifts of up to CHF 100 per person per year from any single source without prior approval. Gifts must be non-cash; gift cards, store credit, and wire transfers are not acceptable regardless of value. Hospitality — meals, events, or entertainment — may be accepted up to a value of CHF 150 per person when the occasion has a clear and legitimate business purpose. Gifts or entertainment exceeding these thresholds require written approval from the employee's manager before acceptance. Any unsolicited gift received above the CHF 100 threshold must be reported to HR and either returned to the sender or donated to a charitable cause.

Employees who are involved in vendor selection, contract negotiation, or procurement decisions are subject to enhanced scrutiny. Any personal relationship — social or financial — with a representative of a vendor under consideration must be disclosed to the Ethics Committee before the employee participates in any related decision or recommendation.

## Outside Employment and Directorships

Employees may pursue outside employment, consulting work, or advisory roles provided these do not compete with the company, involve the use of confidential company information, or interfere with their ability to fulfil their primary responsibilities. Written disclosure to HR is required before commencing any such role. Board membership of a for-profit company requires prior written approval from the Chief Executive. Advisory roles at non-profit or academic institutions do not require approval but must still be disclosed if they are expected to exceed four hours per week.

## Financial Interests

A direct or indirect financial interest in a competitor, supplier, or customer must be disclosed promptly to HR and the employee's line manager. Passive holdings below one percent of a publicly traded company's outstanding shares are generally exempt from disclosure. Any interest in a privately held company that has or is seeking a business relationship with the company must always be disclosed, regardless of the size of the holding.

## Disclosure Procedure

Conflicts must be disclosed in writing to the employee's line manager and to HR as soon as the situation arises or becomes known to the employee. Disclosures are recorded in the conflict of interest register maintained by the Legal and Compliance function. Upon receiving a disclosure, the Ethics Committee will review the matter within ten working days and determine whether a management measure is required. Measures may include recusal from a particular decision, reassignment of responsibility, divestment of a financial interest, or in serious cases, disciplinary action.

## Cooling-off Periods

Employees leaving a role that involved significant procurement authority, vendor management, or regular access to commercially sensitive information are subject to a six-month cooling-off period following their departure. During this period they may not accept employment with, or provide consulting or advisory services to, any supplier or competitor with whom they had material dealings in the twelve months prior to their departure. This obligation applies regardless of the reason for departure and is separate from any non-compete clause contained in the employment contract.

## Role of the Ethics Committee

The Ethics Committee comprises the Chief Compliance Officer, the Head of Human Resources, and an independent non-executive director. It is responsible for reviewing conflict of interest disclosures, determining appropriate management measures, and maintaining the conflict of interest register. The Committee meets at least quarterly and convenes on an ad-hoc basis when a disclosure requires prompt review. All decisions are documented and communicated to the relevant employee in writing within ten working days of the Committee's determination. Questions regarding this policy should be directed to compliance@company.com.
