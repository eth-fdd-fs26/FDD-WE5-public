# Intellectual Property Rights and Proprietary Inventions Framework

## 1. Assignment of Rights and Invention Disclosures
All software code, cryptographic algorithms, hardware architectures, user interfaces, and technical documentation developed by engineering personnel during their contractual employment belong exclusively to the enterprise. Staff innovators are bound to submit an official Invention Disclosure Form via the IP-Master repository immediately upon conceptualizing a patentable solution, novel mechanism, or unique architecture. This filing must happen prior to any external publication, public demonstration, or open-source distribution to preserve absolute novelty and ensure trade secret integrity. Residual rights for innovations developed entirely outside working hours without corporate infrastructure must be vetted by the legal department to establish clear ownership boundaries.

## 2. Evaluation Timelines and Patent Application Procedures
The Intellectual Property Committee, comprising the Chief Technology Officer, product managers, and retained legal counsel, reviews disclosures on a 90-day cycle. If the committee validates the commercial potential of an innovation, the legal team initiates a formal patent application through the Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property. A strict 6-month window is established from disclosure receipt to official patent submission to secure global priority rights. During this interim period, all technical details remain protected under strict trade secret classification, with access logs on relevant source code repositories restricted to the core engineering team.

## 3. Inventor Rewards and Financial Incentives
To foster corporate innovation, the enterprise operates an Inventor Reward Scheme. Upon successful submission of a provisional patent application to the IPI, the contributing engineering team receives a lump-sum inventor bonus of 5,000 CHF, split equally among named contributors. An additional secondary payment of 10,000 CHF triggers if the patent officially grants within any target jurisdiction, such as the European Patent Office or USPTO. Non-disclosure agreements protect these assets, and staff retain a 20% royalty share only on independent commercial licensing agreements explicitly carved out by executive decree, provided they remain with the firm for at least 24 months post-grant.

## 4. Copyright Protection and Open-Source Compliance
Software packages utilizing third-party open-source components must comply with licensing parameters before production integration. Engineering teams are required to execute automated license scanning via the BlackDuck tool prior to every major release cycle. Components governed by restrictive copyleft licenses, such as GPL v3, face an automatic block and require architectural restructuring unless an explicit waiver is granted by the legal board. Documentation, user guides, and promotional graphics are automatically protected under corporate copyright from the moment of creation, requiring the standard corporate copyright notice to be embedded in all digital footers.
