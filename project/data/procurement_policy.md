# Procurement Policy

This policy governs how the company selects suppliers, issues purchase orders, approves expenditure, and manages vendor relationships. It applies to all employees who procure goods or services on behalf of the company. Its purpose is to ensure value for money, maintain competitive and ethical purchasing practices, and manage supply-chain risk.

## Principles

All procurement activity must be conducted with integrity: on merit, transparently, and without discrimination or personal bias toward any vendor. Employees must not award or influence a procurement decision in favour of a vendor in which they or a close associate have a financial interest — this must be declared as a conflict of interest under the Code of Conduct before any involvement.

The company seeks to work with suppliers whose values align with its own, including on environmental standards, labour practices, and data protection. For significant new suppliers, a supplier due diligence questionnaire must be completed before contract signature.

## Spending Thresholds and Approvals

All purchases require advance approval before a commitment is made to a vendor. The approval level depends on the total contract value (including all optional renewal periods):

| Contract value | Approval required |
|---|---|
| Up to CHF 2,000 | Direct manager |
| CHF 2,001 – CHF 15,000 | Manager + Department Head |
| CHF 15,001 – CHF 100,000 | Manager + Department Head + Finance Director |
| Above CHF 100,000 | As above + CEO approval |

These thresholds apply to the total value of the engagement, not a single invoice. Splitting a purchase into multiple smaller orders to avoid a threshold (contract splitting) is prohibited and is treated as a serious violation of this policy.

## Competitive Tendering

The following minimum number of quotes must be obtained before a purchase is approved:

- Up to CHF 5,000: one quote (single source acceptable with justification)
- CHF 5,001 – CHF 25,000: at least two written quotes
- Above CHF 25,000: at least three written quotes from separate, independent vendors

For contracts above CHF 100,000, a formal Request for Proposal (RFP) process must be run, coordinated with the Procurement team. The RFP must define evaluation criteria in advance and document the scoring of each bidder.

Sole-source procurement (awarding without competition) above CHF 5,000 is permitted only in documented exceptional circumstances:
- There is only one capable supplier in the market (natural monopoly or patent)
- Urgency due to an unforeseen emergency makes tendering impractical
- The requirement is for continuation of existing work where a switch of supplier would cause unacceptable disruption or loss of institutional knowledge

Sole-source justifications above CHF 25,000 require Finance Director approval and are subject to post-hoc audit.

## Purchase Orders

All purchases above CHF 500 must be covered by a formal Purchase Order (PO) raised in the finance system before goods are received or services commence. No employee may authorise work to begin or accept delivery without a PO in place, unless a pre-approved exception applies (see Emergency Purchases below).

A PO constitutes a legal commitment to the vendor. Raising a PO without the required approval, or for a value exceeding the approved amount, is a policy violation. If a vendor's invoice does not match the PO (different amount, different scope), the invoice must not be paid until the discrepancy is resolved — do not approve a "close enough" match without written manager and finance approval.

## Contracts

All contracts with a value above CHF 2,000 or a term longer than one month must be reviewed by Legal before signature. Standard vendor terms must never be signed without Legal review, regardless of how routine the vendor presents them. This is especially important for:

- Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) agreements, which often contain broad data processing and IP assignment clauses
- Any agreement touching personal data, which requires a Data Processing Agreement reviewed under GDPR/nDSG requirements
- Agreements with exclusivity provisions or automatic renewal clauses

Only the following individuals have authority to sign contracts on behalf of the company: CEO, CFO, General Counsel, and (for contracts within their department up to CHF 25,000) Department Heads with written CFO authorisation. An employee who signs a contract without proper authority may be held personally liable for the resulting obligation.

Contracts must be filed in the company's contract management system (ContractVault) within five business days of signature. Physical originals must be stored with Legal.

## Preferred Suppliers

The company maintains a list of Preferred Suppliers in categories such as IT hardware, software licences, office supplies, travel, legal services, and recruitment. For these categories, employees should use the preferred supplier by default unless they have a documented reason not to (for example, the preferred supplier cannot meet a specific technical requirement). Preferred suppliers have been negotiated for price, quality, and contractual terms; bypassing them without reason wastes prior commercial effort and may void negotiated pricing.

## Vendor Management

### Ongoing Performance

Vendors providing significant or ongoing services (contracts above CHF 50,000 per year) should be reviewed at least annually. The review should assess delivery quality, responsiveness, value for money, and compliance with contract terms and data protection obligations. Reviews are documented in ContractVault.

### Vendor Risk

Before onboarding a vendor who will process company or customer data, the Procurement team must complete a vendor security assessment. The assessment checks the vendor's certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2), data processing agreements, and subprocessor controls. Vendors with significant security gaps must remediate before contract signature or accept compensating contractual controls.

### Terminating Vendors

When terminating a vendor relationship, the employee responsible for the contract must notify Legal at least as early as the contractual notice period requires. Failure to notify in time can result in automatic contract renewal and unwanted expense. ContractVault sends automated reminders 90 and 30 days before any contract renewal date.

## Emergency Purchases

Where a genuine operational emergency requires a purchase without the normal approval and PO process, the following applies:

1. The purchase may proceed on verbal approval from the direct manager, recorded in writing (email or message) within one hour.
2. A formal PO must be raised retrospectively within two business days.
3. A written explanation of the emergency must be filed with Finance within five business days.
4. Emergency purchases above CHF 10,000 require retrospective Finance Director approval in addition.

Frequent use of the emergency purchase route by the same individual or department will trigger a review by Finance.
