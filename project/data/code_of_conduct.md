# Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct describes the standards of professional behaviour expected of every person who works at or represents the company — employees, contractors, interns, board members, and anyone else acting in a company capacity. It exists to protect individuals, preserve the company's integrity, and create a workplace where everyone can do their best work.

## Respect and Inclusion

Every person has the right to a workplace free from harassment, discrimination, and hostility. We treat colleagues, clients, partners, and the public with dignity regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, marital status, national origin, pregnancy, race, religion, sexual orientation, socioeconomic background, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law.

Harassment includes unwanted physical contact, sexual advances, jokes or comments that demean a person based on who they are, persistent unwanted communication, and deliberate exclusion or "icing out" of individuals. It applies in all work contexts: offices, video calls, conferences, company social events, and work-related communications on personal platforms.

If you witness harassment, you are encouraged — though not required — to intervene safely or report it. Bystander intervention, even simply checking in with the person targeted after the fact, matters.

## Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest arises when your personal interests — financial, relational, or otherwise — could impair, or appear to impair, your ability to act in the company's best interest.

You must disclose conflicts of interest promptly to your manager and HR. Common situations requiring disclosure include:

- Having a financial stake in a company that is a customer, supplier, or competitor
- Recruiting or managing a close family member or romantic partner
- Receiving gifts, hospitality, or other benefits from a vendor or client beyond the Gifts and Entertainment threshold (see below)
- Pursuing an outside business activity that competes with or diverts time from your role
- Serving on the board of a for-profit company without prior approval

Disclosure does not automatically mean a conflict is prohibited. In many cases the company will put appropriate safeguards in place (recusing you from a decision, for example). What is never acceptable is participating in a decision while concealing a conflict.

## Gifts and Entertainment

Exchanging modest gifts and hospitality is normal in business relationships. The following rules ensure these exchanges do not create obligations or the appearance of impropriety.

You may give or receive gifts of up to CHF 100 in value per person per year without prior approval. Gifts must be non-cash (no gift cards, no wire transfers). You may attend client or vendor meals, events, or entertainment of up to CHF 150 per person without prior approval, provided the primary purpose is a legitimate business discussion.

Gifts or entertainment above these thresholds require written manager approval before acceptance or giving. Cash gifts, loans, or any benefit tied explicitly or implicitly to a business decision must always be refused. If you receive an unsolicited gift that exceeds the threshold, inform your manager and return or donate it.

Never give anything to a public official, civil servant, or government-owned entity's employee without first consulting Legal. Different legal regimes (Swiss law, FCPA, UK Bribery Act) apply depending on jurisdiction, and even small gifts can constitute bribery.

## Confidentiality

Employees have access to confidential company information — financial results, customer data, product plans, personnel details — as a function of their work. This information must not be shared outside the company without authorisation, or inside the company beyond those with a genuine business need.

Confidentiality obligations survive employment. If you leave the company, you remain bound by these obligations with respect to information obtained during your employment. Downloading or retaining confidential information before departure (on personal devices, cloud storage, or email) without authorisation is a violation of this policy and may expose you to legal action.

During any business discussions with third parties, employees must not disclose confidential information before an appropriate non-disclosure agreement is in place. Legal can turn these around quickly when required.

## Insider Trading

If your role gives you access to material non-public information (MNPI) — for example, unannounced financial results, a pending acquisition, or a major contract win or loss — you must not trade in the company's securities or the securities of counterparties, and must not tip others to do so. This applies to you personally, to family members you live with, and to entities you control.

MNPI is information a reasonable investor would consider significant in deciding to buy or sell. If you are unsure whether information qualifies, treat it as MNPI and consult Legal before trading. The company maintains a list of individuals who are in "closed periods" during which trading is always prohibited. If you are on that list, you will be notified.

## Accurate Records

Every employee is responsible for ensuring that any record they create or contribute to — financial, legal, operational, or otherwise — is accurate, complete, and not misleading. This includes expense reports, time records, customer contracts, quality certifications, and any regulatory filings.

Deliberately falsifying records, altering documents without authorisation, or omitting material facts from reports is fraud. It is grounds for immediate termination and may result in criminal charges. If you are ever asked by a manager or colleague to record something you know to be inaccurate, you must refuse and report the request to the Compliance team or via the whistleblowing channel.

## Use of Company Resources

Company resources — equipment, software, premises, time, and funds — are provided to carry out company business. Incidental personal use is acceptable when it is minor and does not interfere with work. The following are not acceptable:

- Using company resources for a personal or third-party business
- Taking company property for personal use outside the workplace without approval
- Excessive personal use of the internet, phone, or equipment during working hours
- Disposing of company property without IT or Facilities approval

## Compliance with Laws

All employees must comply with applicable laws and regulations in the jurisdictions where they work and where the company operates. This includes competition law (no price-fixing, market allocation, or bid-rigging discussions with competitors), anti-corruption law, data protection law (GDPR and Swiss nDSG), and export controls.

If you are uncertain whether an activity is lawful, consult Legal. "I didn't know it was illegal" is not a defence, but "I asked and was told incorrectly" is a far better position than having acted without asking.

## Speaking Up and Non-Retaliation

The company depends on employees raising concerns when something seems wrong. You are encouraged to speak to your manager, HR, or Legal directly. If you are not comfortable with direct channels, you may use the anonymous whistleblowing hotline at whistleblower@company.com or the third-party platform linked on the intranet.

Retaliation against anyone who raises a genuine concern in good faith — regardless of whether the concern turns out to be substantiated — is prohibited and is itself a disciplinary offence. Deliberate false reporting intended to harm a colleague is equally a violation of this policy.

## Consequences of Violations

Breaches of this Code of Conduct may result in disciplinary action ranging from a written warning to termination of employment, depending on severity, intent, and whether the conduct is repeated. Serious violations may be referred to law enforcement.

The Code of Conduct does not override employment law, and disciplinary proceedings will follow applicable legal requirements and the company's HR procedures.

Questions about this Code should be directed to compliance@company.com.
