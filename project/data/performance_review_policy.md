# Performance Review Policy

This policy describes how the company evaluates employee performance, how reviews are conducted, and how performance connects to compensation, development, and employment decisions. It applies to all permanent employees who have passed their probation period. Contractors and temporary workers are not subject to this policy.

## Purpose of Performance Reviews

Performance reviews serve three distinct but connected purposes. First, they give employees structured, honest feedback so they understand how their work is perceived and where they can improve. Second, they provide a documented basis for compensation decisions, promotions, and — in serious cases — performance improvement processes. Third, they align individual goals with team and company objectives, so each person understands how their work contributes to the bigger picture.

The company does not use performance reviews purely as a formality. Managers are expected to invest genuine time in preparation, be candid, and be specific. Vague or uniformly positive reviews that do not reflect reality harm the employee, the team, and the company's ability to develop its people.

## Review Cycles

### Annual Review

A formal annual review takes place in December, covering performance from January to December of the current year. Outcomes of the annual review inform compensation changes effective 1 February of the following year. Employees who join after 30 June participate in the annual review for goal-setting purposes only, with their first full performance rating deferred to the following year.

### Mid-Year Check-in

An informal mid-year check-in takes place in June. This is not a rating event — no formal performance score is recorded — but it is a required conversation in which the manager and employee review progress against goals set in January, identify any development areas, and adjust goals if circumstances have materially changed.

### Probation Review

New employees undergo a formal review at the end of their probation period (typically three months). The probation review is separate from the annual cycle and determines whether probation is passed, extended, or terminated. Its criteria and process are described in the Probation Policy.

## Goal Setting

In January each year, each employee and their manager agree on five to seven goals for the year. Goals must be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Goals are recorded in HRConnect and are visible to the employee, their manager, and the HR Business Partner.

Goals fall into two categories:

**Performance goals** — Outcomes tied to the employee's core responsibilities. A sales manager's goal might be a specific revenue target; an engineer's might be delivering a set of features by a given date.

**Development goals** — Skills, behaviours, or knowledge areas the employee is building. These are just as important as performance goals, and managers should devote time to supporting them.

At least one goal per employee must relate to collaboration or cross-functional impact — a deliberate check against purely individual-focused objectives.

## Rating Framework

At the annual review, the manager assigns a performance rating on a five-point scale:

**5 — Exceptional**: Consistently delivers well above role expectations. Sets a clear positive example for the team. Outcomes achieved have high impact beyond the immediate role.

**4 — Exceeds Expectations**: Delivers above role expectations on most goals. Demonstrates strong initiative and is relied upon beyond formal responsibilities.

**3 — Meets Expectations**: Delivers on all or nearly all goals to the expected standard. Performs reliably and consistently in the role. The majority of the workforce falls here; it is an explicitly positive rating.

**2 — Partially Meets Expectations**: Delivers on some but not all goals, or delivers all goals below the expected standard. A development plan must be initiated within 30 days of this rating.

**1 — Does Not Meet Expectations**: Consistently fails to deliver. Typically results in a formal Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) within 30 days of this rating.

Calibration sessions are held across peer managers before ratings are finalised to ensure consistency. No forced distribution is applied — there is no mandated percentage at each rating level — but managers must be prepared to justify outlier ratings (many 5s or many 1s from one manager will prompt a calibration conversation with HR).

## The Review Conversation

The annual review conversation must take place in person or via video call — not by written message alone. It should last at least 45 minutes. The manager should not read out a pre-written review without dialogue; the conversation is expected to be two-way, with the employee having space to share their own perspective.

Before the meeting, the employee completes a self-assessment in HRConnect covering their progress against each goal, key achievements, challenges faced, and how they would like to develop next year. The manager reads this before the conversation, not during it.

Managers must give at least one example for each significant development area raised — vague feedback such as "you need to communicate better" without a concrete example is insufficient and will be returned by HR for revision.

After the meeting, the manager writes the formal review in HRConnect within five business days. The employee then acknowledges the review electronically. Acknowledgement means the employee has read the review; it does not mean they agree. Employees may add their own comments to the record.

## Link to Compensation

Salary reviews are informed by performance ratings but are not mechanical. A rating of 3 (Meets Expectations) qualifies the employee for a cost-of-living adjustment. Ratings of 4 and 5 make the employee eligible for merit increases above cost-of-living; actual amounts depend on budget, market data, and position within salary band. Ratings of 1 or 2 result in no salary increase and, typically, a freeze on variable pay.

Compensation decisions are made by the manager in collaboration with HR and department leadership. Employees are informed of compensation changes in January, effective 1 February.

## Promotions

A promotion to a higher level or band requires a sustained record of performance at the higher level, not just one exceptional year. Managers nominate candidates during the December review cycle, and nominations are reviewed by a cross-functional Promotions Committee in January. Employees who feel they are performing above their level and have not been nominated may raise this with their manager or HR Business Partner.

## Performance Improvement Plans

An employee receiving a rating of 1 or 2 will be placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) unless there are specific documented circumstances (for example, a serious personal event that affected performance). A PIP is a formal, written document that:

- States clearly the specific performance shortfalls observed
- Sets measurable improvement targets for a defined review period (typically 60 to 90 days)
- Specifies the support the company will provide (coaching, training, adjusted workload)
- States the consequences if targets are not met, including potential termination

A PIP is not a precursor to termination — it is a genuine attempt to support the employee in improving. Employees on a PIP receive weekly check-ins with their manager and have access to an HR Business Partner for support. All PIP documents are stored in HRConnect and are treated as Confidential.

## Appeals

An employee who believes their performance rating is unfair may appeal by writing to their HR Business Partner within 10 business days of acknowledging their review. The HR Business Partner will review the evidence, interview the manager, and may convene a panel if the matter is complex. The appeal decision is final.
